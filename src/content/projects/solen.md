problem: "I was interested in that in-between territory — not fine, but not serious enough to go looking for help. The question I kept coming back to: what would software look like if it made room for that state instead of trying to immediately fix it or measure it?"
approach: "So far the built product is still in its early stages. The idea is a room-like environment that moves through stages — arrival, settling, deepening, lingering, drifting — and through states like neutral, calm, steady, heavy, and storm, with the environment itself changing rather than adding more screens or notifications. The visual direction leans toward wabi-sabi and deep minimalism. I've also been thinking about what kind of support might fit inside it — an AI companion, fragments of other people, or something closer to a human anchor — though none of that is implemented yet."
outcome: "Working through Solen changed how I think about interfaces: a meaningful shift in an experience doesn't have to be a new screen or button; the environment itself can change instead. Beyond that, most of what I'd call a real lesson is still ahead of me."
---

Solen is a concept for a digital environment built around emotional state rather than features or screens. It's meant for the quieter, harder-to-name territory between being completely fine and needing serious intervention — not therapy, not meditation, not another productivity or self-improvement system.
